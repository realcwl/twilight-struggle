# NATO — illustration direction

The selected V2 01 frame remains the foundation: US blue enamel, raised gold edge,
a black slanted title bar, ivory rules, and the shared neutral black/gold back.
This card adapts the user-supplied Twilight Struggle card 21, rather than replacing
any of the 81 original event concepts in `deck/`.

## Illustration

Saved artwork: [`signing-1949.png`](signing-1949.png), 1254 × 1254.
Card exports: [`front.png`](front.png) and [`back.png`](back.png), 1500 × 2100.
The complete source text is in [`card.js`](card.js), and the interactive page is
[`../nato.html`](../nato.html). The front's smaller picture window and taller ivory
plate make room for both protection clauses and the prerequisite without shortening
the original rules. The asterisk, title underline, 4 Ops and US / Early War labels
are retained. All typography and framing remain editable HTML/SVG/CSS.

Generated with the built-in imagegen tool. The scene is an interpretive historical
painting, not an archival photograph or an exact reconstruction of a particular
photograph. The signing in Washington on 4 April 1949 is documented by the
[US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/featured-documents/north-atlantic-treaty).
The NATO compass emblem belongs to the card's graphic frame, not the 1949 scene:
it was adopted in 1953, as recorded in the
[NATO Handbook](https://archives.nato.int/uploads/r/null/1/4/145732/0050_NATO_Handbook_1992_ENG.pdf).

### Final generation prompt

Use case: historical-scene.
Asset type: square illustration for a circular gold-framed window on a Cold War strategy card titled NATO. Generate only the painting, not a card or mockup.
Primary request: a slightly realistic, immersive historical painting evoking the signing of the North Atlantic Treaty in Washington, 4 April 1949.
Scene: a dignified late-1940s diplomatic auditorium, dark wood table, tall warm stone pilasters and softly draped national flags in the background. A seated middle-aged diplomat in a charcoal wool suit leans forward to sign an open ivory treaty with a fountain pen. His face in three-quarter profile, writing hand, pen and paper form one clear central focal group. Two suited delegates stand behind him, softly resolved but anatomically credible, witnessing the signing. Suggest an international gathering without a crowded group portrait.
Style: premium historical board-game illustration; convincing human proportions, textured wool, paper and wood, restrained oil-painted brushwork, carefully observed light and shadow. More lifelike and dimensional than a flat propaganda poster, but still a coherent painted illustration rather than a glossy photograph or 3D render.
Composition: square image, close medium shot from table height, slightly angled perspective. All essential faces, writing hand and open treaty comfortably inside the central 70 percent so a circular crop preserves the narrative. A confident readable silhouette at small card size. Full-bleed background to all four corners, no circular mask or borders.
Light and palette: soft late-afternoon window light from the left, subtle amber highlights on the paper and faces, deep ink-blue and charcoal shadows, desaturated cobalt cloth, warm ivory stone. Restrained color harmonizing with blue enamel, aged gold, cream and black.
Constraints: plausible 1949 suits and objects. No modern NATO flag or compass emblem in the historical scene, no modern maple-leaf Canadian flag, no modern technology, no weapons, no crowds of tiny faces. No readable lettering, signatures, title, typography, logos, frame or watermark. Hands must be natural; the pen touches the document. No cartoon features, flat vector shapes, halftone dots, excessive grunge, orange-teal cinematic grading, fantasy spectacle or photoreal plastic skin.
